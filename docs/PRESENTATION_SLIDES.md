## Slide 2: The problem

> Banks receive thousands of loan applications. Checking each one by hand is slow, inconsistent and subjective.

> Mistake 1: approve a risky applicant

> The customer defaults and the bank loses money (a false positive).

> Mistake 2: reject a good applicant

> The bank loses a good customer and the business (a false negative).

> OUR GOAL

> Learn from past applications and predict Approved / Rejected automatically, and explain which features drive the decision.

## Slide 6: Features we created: affordability

> A loan officer asks: "Can this household afford the monthly instalment?" So we built 5 new columns that answer it.

> 1

> TotalIncome

> Applicant + Co-applicant income

> The bank lends to the household

> 2

> EMI

> LoanAmount × 1000 ÷ Term

> Approx. monthly instalment in ₹

> 3

> BalanceIncome

> TotalIncome − EMI

> Money left after paying the EMI

> 4

> LoanToIncome

> LoanAmount × 1000 ÷ TotalIncome

> Loan size in months of income

> 5

> LoanAmountLog

> log(1 + LoanAmount)

> Tames very large loans

> Did it help?
> LR test ROC-AUC 0.852 → 0.870. LoanToIncome & BalanceIncome are the Random Forest's #2 and #3 columns.

> Computed inside the pipeline (src/features.py), so the app sends only raw values.

**Say:** This is our main modification. The formulas use only the applicant's own row and learn nothing from the data, so there is no leakage. Evidence: LR's ROC-AUC improved and two engineered features ranked right after Credit_History in the forest.

## Slide 8: Three models compared

> Logistic Regression

> Linear baseline

> Weighted sum → sigmoid → probability
> Needs scaled features
> Easy to interpret (weights)

> Decision Tree

> Rule-based (max depth 4)

> Learns yes/no questions using Gini impurity
> Depth capped to avoid memorising
> Human-readable rules

> Random Forest

> Ensemble of 300 trees (new)

> Each tree sees random rows + features
> Trees vote: their errors average out
> Usually beats one tree

> ★ Selected model

> All three share the same features and preprocessing, so the comparison is fair.

**Say:** Sigmoid: the S-curve mapping any score into 0 to 1, read as a probability. Gini: how mixed a node is; the tree picks splits that make nodes purer. Random Forest = bagging + random feature subsets. We added it as a modification.

## Slide 9: How we evaluate fairly

> 5-fold cross-validation (new)

> Gold = validation fold, green = training folds. Five rounds on the 491 training rows; the average F1 picks the winner. The test set is only used once, at the end.

> 1

> Accuracy

> % of all decisions correct (misleading with a 69/31 imbalance)

> 2

> Precision

> Of loans we approved, how many were truly good? Protects the bank

> 3

> Recall

> Of truly good loans, how many did we approve? Protects customers

> 4

> F1-score

> Balance of precision & recall (our selection metric)

> 5

> ROC-AUC

> How well applicants are ranked (0.5 = coin flip, 1 = perfect)

**Say:** Why cross-validation? One test set of 123 rows is a single lucky or unlucky draw. CV gives an average and a spread. We pick the model on CV F1 so the test set never influences the choice.
